Act as a senior brand strategist running a paid discovery session with a new client. I'm the client. Interview me, then write my Brand Discovery Brief. This brief will be the foundation for my name, logo, colors, fonts and voice, so do it properly.

My starting point: [business name or "no name yet"], [what I sell], [who buys it], [how long I've been doing it], [what I charge, roughly].

Interview me in five rounds. One question at a time. Wait for each answer.
Round 1, the business: what I sell, what problem it solves, what a customer's life looks like before and after, why someone would pick me over doing nothing.
Round 2, the people: my best customer ever (describe them), my worst customer ever, where my customers hang out online and offline, what they're tired of hearing.
Round 3, the competition: who else they could buy from, what those brands get right, what they all do that's boring or annoying.
Round 4, the personality: if my brand walked into a bar, who would it be; three words I want people to use about me; three words I never want to hear; brands I love from any industry and why.
Round 5, the practical stuff: budget, deadlines, where the brand shows up first (Facebook, website, truck wrap, packaging), anything I'm keeping (an old logo, a color I'm attached to).

Rules: if an answer is vague, ask a follow-up. If I contradict myself, point it out. Don't move to the next round until you understand this one.

Then write the brief with these headings: The Business in One Paragraph, The Why (draft), Ideal Customer, Competitors and the Sea of Sameness, Personality (3 traits, plus 3 anti-traits), Visual Direction Notes, Must-Haves and Never-Evers, Open Questions. End with the 3 biggest risks to this brand, stated bluntly.
