Where this came from

LIFE IS SHORT.
MAKE IT MAGICAL.

That was the line on the original AntiHero channel art: white label boxes stacked over a dark, sun-bleached beach. It covered fitness, travel, lifestyle, and drones.

The badge did the real work. A circle-A that trades half its ring for the word hero. It said: you don’t have to wait for one. You can be one, on your own terms.

AntiHero now trains a different muscle: AI tools, branding, search, and the unglamorous work of building a business. Same attitude. New weights.

The rebuild

FROM A BLURRY SCREENSHOT TO CLEAN VECTOR

The only surviving copy of the logo was a small, compressed avatar. It has been rebuilt: the circle-A from measured geometry, the lettering traced and redrawn as smooth curves. It now scales from a 16 px favicon to a billboard.

After: rebuilt vector, same crop

Faithful. The rebuilt mark matches the original within 0.6% of its pixels, so it reads as the same logo.

Systematic. One stroke weight everywhere (0.158 of the radius), and a diagonal fixed at 24.7°.

ANTIHERO / BRAND BIBLE02 Positioning09 / 42
The anti-hero idea

HEROES WAIT FOR THE CALL. ANTI-HEROES ANSWER THEIR OWN.

The internet is full of people selling you a savior: the course, the guru, the secret. AntiHero is the opposite. Nobody here is going to save you, and that is the good news.

You are the main character. The group is the crew: people learning in public, sharing what works, and pulling the next person up. AI is the new gear: it gives one person the reach of a small team, if they learn to use it.

The badge says it without words. The circle-A is the old sign for going your own way. Half the circle is missing, and the word hero completes it. The story isn’t finished until you write yourself into it.

YOU COMPLETE THE CIRCLE.

Brand pillars

FOUR THINGS WE ALWAYS DO

01

OWN YOUR STORY

Agency over everything. Nobody is coming. Good. Every post ends with something the reader can do today.

02

TOOLS OVER TALK

Show the prompt, the workflow, and the result. Practical beats inspirational, every time.

03

STRAIGHT TALK

No hype, no fake screenshots, no overnight promises. If something is hard or slow, say so.

04

BUILD IN PUBLIC

Share the work, the numbers, and the mistakes. The whole group learns faster that way.

When in doubt, test a post against the pillars. If it doesn’t hand the reader agency, a tool, the truth, or the real work behind it, cut it.

MECHANICS

No exclamation points. Oxford comma. Numbers as numerals. Headlines in caps (Bebas Neue), body in sentence case. “AI” is always capitalized. Say you, not users. Say build, not hustle.

DOThis took me three weekends. Here’s what broke.
DON’TMake $10K a month on autopilot with this one tool.

DON’TRank #1 on Google overnight, guaranteed.

WORDS WE USE

build, ship, tools, test, learn, own, story, plain, real, crew, receipts

WORDS WE AVOID

hustle, passive income, guru, crush it, secret, game-changer, revolutionary

Big numbers need receipts. “I wrote 40 product descriptions before lunch” beats any multiplier. Keep it fun: the work is serious, the tone doesn’t have to be.

ANTIHERO / BRAND BIBLE05 Color24 / 42
Rules

THE ONE RED THING RULE

Every layout gets one red idea: a word, a button, a label, or the hero in the wordmark. Two red things compete. Zero is fine.

  • Pair a bright, open scene with dark type: black label boxes and panels over light imagery, white boxes over dark grounds.
  • Red is never a background for long text, and never a full-page wash.
  • On red, everything is white: the wordmark too.
  • Keep photography bright and natural. If the picture already carries red (the balloon, the red plane), the red tag may echo it; nothing else goes red.
  • Ember only appears as a pressed or hover state.

ANTIHERO / BRAND BIBLE06 Type28 / 42
The signature device

THE LABEL SYSTEM

Straight from the original channel art: Bebas Neue set on solid boxes and stacked like a warning label. It is the fastest way to make anything look like AntiHero.

  • Over bright imagery: black boxes with white text. Over dark grounds: white boxes with black text. One red box per layout.
  • Padding: 0.14 × the font size top and bottom, 0.2 left and right.
  • Stack boxes flush, with no gap. Let the widths step.
  • Keep the stack clear of the picture’s subject (the balloon, the plane). Left-align on covers and posts.
  • Pair with the flanking rules: two hairlines either side of a short line.

Imagery direction

RISE ABOVE, LIGHT FIRST

Every picture should feel like a morning: open sky, altitude, and a sense of getting out. The metaphors are flight and freedom: rising, launching, leaving the formation, seeing further.

SHOOT + USESunrise skies, cloud seas, balloons, paper planes, drones, open roads, summits, real people building real things in daylight. Technology that looks friendly and useful.
SKIPDark “hacker” tech, glowing circuit brains, robots, stock smiles, handshakes, rented supercars, cash fans, and anything that sells a lifestyle instead of a skill.

TREATMENT

Keep it bright and natural, a touch warm. Fine grain is fine; heavy filters are not. Dark comes from the type: black label boxes and panels on top of the light. One red element carries the colour.

ANTIHERO / BRAND BIBLE09 The family40 / 42
Co-branding rules

GIVE FIRST. ASK RARELY.

  1. The community is the product. AntiHero leads everything members see. It gives away real value until people ask how to work with you.
  2. Nine to one. About nine posts of pure value for every post that points to KR8TIV or KR8TIV AI. The ask is an invitation or a case study, never a pitch in someone’s DMs.
  3. Teach with real work. “How we automated a crew’s back office” is a lesson first and a KR8TIV AI case study second.
  4. One colour inside AntiHero. On AntiHero layouts the sister logos appear single-colour (white or black), never in their own accents, so the one-red rule holds.
  5. Each brand keeps its own look on its own site. Optional: the studios can point back with the “Part of the AntiHero family” endorsement in their footers.
  6. One founder voice. Matt signs the posts. No corporate “we” in the group.
